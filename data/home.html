Jazz

The tide breathes in and out, sand black as night. At each inhalation, the rocks filter its breath, crackling together, grinding away. It sounds like the applause I got after my lecture on jazz: the improvisational techniques of the greats, you know, Coltrane, Jarrett, Lloyd, Garcia. Naturally, I strolled onto stage with no plan, no notes, no slides, ready to let the pendulum swing. Tête-à-tête. The greats were in my soul right then, producing the room that soon became a shouting match between an evolutionary psychologist and a neo-Marxist, with me giggling into a microphone, allowing only my left leg to peek out from behind the podium I hid behind. Both parties were of course invited by yours truly. We can't improvise without some a priori agreement.

'If one speaks of modernity, one must consider the historical and cultural production it was built on—an acorn falls from a tree, a tree is made, an acorn—'

'That's exactly right. How do you think that production came to be? How many variations and derivations, failed sexings and conditions—'

'Right there! You just said it! We're slaves to our conditions! And who owns those conditions if not the oppressors? The acorn—'

'If nature is the oppressor then that's a mighty fine analysis of what occurs. But don't you worry—time is on the side of nature, nature on the side of itself, and us pr—'

'No, no, no!! Time is evil. Time is on the side of the capitalist, capitalism is everything I hate about the world, thus time must be evil. That's fucking syllogistically effete. Your eyes are shut!'

'You're young and transcendentally miserable. We all had radical roots. I remember a balmy day in '67 when I marched the st—'

'ANSWER THE QUESTION!'

'You didn't ask one.'

The stands soon cleared and they resorted to tantric acts of attack, whilst I bumped jungle beats through the PA system and gurgled into the microphone on the floor. I suppose my opiate intake at this point was out of control. Alas, so was Coltrane's. The crowd stayed for the show, cheering and jeering, placing bets, producing an all-out celebration. Of course they're both right and wrong, but that's of little importance.

The neo-Marxist soon burst into tears, the auditorium echoing and reverberating with cheers and laughter. I opened my eyes and looked at the fluorescent lights above. And the rocks crackled... I mean the people clapped... and I...

I? I press my hand into the dark silt, pushing down until muddy water engulfs it. Tiny grains of sand swirl, hitting the interdigital webs of my hand and bouncing off. Diatomic! If only I had a microscope for eyes and a cut larynx. If only eye were all that was left, to look up and be the stars, watch the pebbles shift.

Sarah comes outside and puts her hand on my shoulder. She doesn't utter a word. A wave comes crashing in and hits us both, engulfing me and slamming her backward. She sputters, I drink.

I drank a glass of water with two Alka-Seltzers that morning. The night prior was a blur of loudness, visual loudness. My eyes were sore. I threw the glass on the floor for no other reason than I could. It fractalized and shone in the light. Again, for no other reason than I could, I stepped on a shard and watched blood maroon away. The cold tile was all I felt. Ice.

Sarah would carry around a cup filled to the brim with ice. She would take one into her mouth and shoot it out like a projectile, aiming for my forehead. My head is a tank of propane. The ice dings. On our third date I took her out to the desert and laid out my collection of rifles on a flat rock. Out of my backpack I pulled four propane tanks and laid them out, one hundred steps apart in the sand. We took turns shooting them, watching them burst into the air, flames exploding everywhere. The tank always takes an explosive arc upwards, its metallic shell sailing through the air before thumping back down in an upright position.

Upright and erect, Ms. Larval would always tell me! One time she caught me smirking and pulled me in front of the class to comp with her. She'd play wrong notes at the most inopportune moments, aware there was no way I could recover. Add a minor third, a major seventh, a diminished sixth, erect my posture.

Real eyes realize reels lie erect on top of the water. The tiniest rivulets circle out and away, chased by a sixth, a third, a seventh, a ninth. No fish are here. I just like the look of the string hiding below. Sarah left. I didn't see her do it. Perhaps I did. The line bobs and sinks.

Blood was exchanged for sweet relief. The needle sank into a particularly purple vein. Gurgle. My larynx left me. The floor vibrated with intensities and rhythms. I tapped a finger to the beat. People shouted and guffawed at the boxing match below.

'Australopithecus!'

'Surplus!'

My organs were stripped bare, everything flowed. What a session this was. Was I on stage or fighting or cheering? All? None? Not one nor the other, perhaps. I crawled deeper under the podium and played my clarinet. My face was ice hot. I rubbed against the grit of the carpet, burning what I had left. Glass shattered in the distance.

'Biodetermine this, bitch!'

'That's not even how that works, you four-eyed ressentiment machine!'

Crackled clapping on the shore. The tide is getting closer. Where is Sarah? Had she left? My body is soaked. I'll take a lie-down, I think. The stars are bright, all I can hear is my eye. Diatoms sparkle below. I sea them. The wave sweeps in.

I have a fleet now swimming from shore to shore. I will drop a twig in as a raft for a drowning sailor. I will drop a stone in and see bubbles rise from the depths of the sea. Neville has gone and Susan has gone; Jinny is in the kitchen garden picking currants with Louis perhaps. I have a short time alone, while Miss Hudson spreads our copy-books on the schoolroom table. I have a short space of freedom. I have picked all the fallen petals and made them swim. I have put raindrops in some. I will plant a lighthouse here, a head of Sweet Alice. And I will now rock the brown basin from side to side so that my ships may ride the waves. Some will founder. Some will dash themselves against the cliffs. One sails alone. That is my ship. It sails into icy caverns where the sea-bear barks and stalactites swing green chains. The waves rise; their crests curl; look at the lights on the mastheads. They have scattered, they have foundered, all except my ship, which mounts the wave and sweeps before the gale and reaches the islands where the parrots chatter and the creepers …”

Virginia Woolf, The Waves
